Lantern Reader

Support

Lantern Reader teaches a child to read English print — the code, not the language. It assumes she already speaks English and is learning how the letters work.

Need a person?

Email lanternreaderapp@gmail.com. Please say which device you are using and what happened just before the problem. Replies usually come within a few days.

Getting started

  1. Open the app and enter your child's first name and age.
  2. Let her do the first assessment. It is short, adapts as it goes, and is deliberately not framed as a test — its only job is to skip past what she already knows.
  3. After that, each session runs about twenty minutes and picks its own content.

You do not need to plan anything. The app decides what to teach next from what she has already shown it.

Common questions

She is not being heard when she reads aloud

Reading aloud needs both the microphone and speech recognition. Check Settings → Lantern Reader on your device and confirm both are switched on. Inside the app, Grown-ups → Check the microphone now tells you whether it is working before a lesson rather than during one.

If it still will not listen, tap Carry on without it. Everything in the app works by tapping, and nothing in her progress record depends on the microphone.

It keeps mishearing her

This is common with a child's voice, and more so with an accent the recogniser was not trained on. The app is built to be forgiving, but if it gets her wrong repeatedly, tap You keep getting me wrong. It will run a short "say it after me" exercise and learn how she actually pronounces things. That profile is kept, and survives a progress reset, because her voice has not changed.

The app never marks a word wrong on the strength of speech recognition alone.

A letter sound does not sound right

Go to Grown-ups → Sound check. Every sound in the app is listed with a play button. The tutor's voice is pre-recorded, and where a recording exists it is what you will hear — the spelling controls on that screen do not change it.

She is stuck, or it is too easy

The app moves on only when she has shown she is secure, so it will not race ahead. If it is clearly too easy, Grown-ups → Redo the assessment re-measures her without erasing what she has learned.

If it is too hard, that usually means a sound underneath is not solid yet. The progress report in the Grown-ups area names the specific patterns she is finding difficult.

Can more than one child use it?

Yes. Each child has a separate profile and a separate record, and one child's answers never affect another's.

Does it work without the internet?

Yes, completely. The app makes no network requests at all. Everything — including the tutor's voice and all the stories — is inside the app itself.

How do I see what she has actually learned?

Grown-ups (top-right) holds the full record: letters, sounds, words and sight words, which ones are secure, which are shaky, and what the app plans to do next. It is behind a PIN so it stays out of a child's reach.

How do I back up, move, or delete her progress?

In Grown-ups → Settings:

  • Export a backup — saves the complete record as a file you keep. Import it on another device to move her progress across.
  • Reset progress — clears what she has learned, keeps the profile.
  • Delete profile — removes the child entirely.

There is no cloud copy of any of this, so a backup file is the only way to move or restore a record. Deleting the app removes everything it stored.

I forgot the Grown-ups PIN

For safety reasons the PIN cannot be recovered from the app. Deleting and reinstalling the app clears it, but also erases all progress — so export a backup first if you can still get in. If you cannot, email us and we will talk you through the options.

What the app deliberately does not do

  • No scores, no stars, no streaks and no red marks. It never says "wrong".
  • No adverts, no purchases, no subscriptions, no sign-up.
  • No leaderboards and nothing social. Nothing is shared with anyone.
  • No time limits or targets. Progress is gated on understanding, never on minutes spent.

Accessibility

The reading typeface is Andika, designed by SIL International for beginning readers — it distinguishes b d p q clearly and uses single-storey a and g, which matters in an app whose job is telling those apart. Every instruction is read aloud, and the replay button repeats both the instruction and the sound.

If something is hard to see, hear or tap, please tell us — that is a bug worth fixing.

Reporting a problem

Email lanternreaderapp@gmail.com with:

  • the device and iOS version
  • what screen you were on
  • what you expected, and what happened instead

The app keeps a short local error log (Grown-ups → Settings) that you can read and include. It contains no personal information — only screen names, error messages and the app version.
